I work in cybersecurity at a US health system, where the work involves cybersecurity engineers, network teams and caregivers. Part of my work there is making cybersecurity understandable to people who do not work in it.

> **Note for Raghunath:** What does "understandable" mean to you, in your own terms?

In this essay, I write about the comic strips and videos I have made, and then about how I think an awareness program should be designed.

## Comic strips for caregivers

In early 2025, there was a need for comic strips on cybersecurity topics, so that busy caregivers could understand a topic by glancing at a strip for a minute or two.

The image-generation tools we had at the time were not good at placing text in images. So I created the comic panels image by image, with Microsoft 365 Copilot's built-in image generation, and then learned Figma to place the dialogue accurately on each panel.

Some of the comic strips were used in a couple of newsletters. The rest were appreciated, but they never got a chance to be used at clinical sites, because of some limitations there.

## Videos for an internal cybersecurity platform

I have also scripted and produced product videos for an internal cybersecurity platform, from storyboard to motion graphics in Premiere Pro and After Effects.

In my view, videos are necessary in a world full of LLMs.

> **Note for Raghunath:** Your sentence states the view but not the reason. Why are videos necessary in a world full of LLMs, and does that apply to the product videos you made?

## Cyber awareness as a drill

What follows are my views; I have not put them into practice in my organization.

Most enterprises treat cyber awareness as a drill; they keep pushing training and phishing drills. Phishing drills are okay, but pushing training only overburdens people.

What I believe in is a careful, thoughtfully designed skills survey, and requesting training completion based on role, skill level and necessity.

For traditionally run awareness programs, this is much harder to do. The people with those responsibilities will keep doing what they have been doing: declaring October cyber awareness month, conducting some fun events, and pushing some emails, sessions and trainings.

I am surprised that people do not put more thought into the structural thinking I am trying to champion.
