## Thesis

Over the past few months, I have observed one peculiarity with AI, especially LLMs (as future AI systems may grow beyond LLMs): it relies on attributing mathematical logic and relationships to the language and its intricacies, and it then reapplies this gigantic mathematical modeling in interacting with the users, both agentic users and humans.

All of this translates to learning patterns and reapplying them, based on mathematics-powered computational logic.

The language itself has a nuance: it is not born out of rules. It is born out of human evolution and humans becoming societal. Then rules are derived from known literature and language artifacts for learning purposes and standardization.

This paradigm of language means that it evolves continuously and changes direction at a different pace at different times. This is well noted by Bryan Garner's *Garner's Modern English Usage*.[^garner]

During the LLM training phase, I presume, the model is trained on language patterns created during different times, with many English language words taking different meanings. The human societies themselves in different geographies interpret word meaning and word combinations (phrases) in different ways.

This doesn't have to mean an AI model should be restricted to using words or phrases that only have concrete meanings, with a strong assumption that most human societies understand them as concrete without meaning drift.

This is only for my use case, where I want the agentic AI systems to communicate back or write in words, phrases, and sentence lengths that carry concrete meaning and information to the maximum possible extent, and stay honest to the current times at which the model or AI system is responding.

## Outline

### 1. The peculiarity I noticed

- Where the observation comes from: months of working with language models, directly and through agents.
- The observation itself: the model treats language as mathematical logic and relationships, learns those relationships, and reapplies them to whoever is on the other side, a person or another agent.

### 2. What the model does with your words

- The tokenizer splits text into tokens. Each model provider has its own tokenizer and vocabulary.
- Each token becomes a vector of numbers, from a lookup table the model learned in training.
- Inside the transformer, attention works out how each token relates to the others, and the feed-forward layers are where much of the learned knowledge is believed to live. Mostly matrix multiplication, repeated across many layers.
- At the end, the model scores every possible next token; softmax turns the scores into probabilities, and sampling picks one, shaped by temperature, top-k and top-p.
- Then it repeats, one token at a time, until the answer is done.
- The point to make here: every step is mathematics applied to patterns found in text.

### 3. Where language comes from

- Not from rules. From human evolution, and from people becoming societal.
- The rules come afterwards: derived from known literature and language artifacts, for learning and standardization.
- Language keeps evolving, at a different pace at different times. *Garner's Modern English Usage* as the record of that.

### 4. What the training data carries

- Patterns written at different times, so one English word arrives with several meanings.
- The same words and phrases, read differently in different geographies and societies.
- What follows, as I presume it: the model learns all of this as relationships and reapplies it when it answers.

### 5. Meaning drift

When you explain something to a T, the model will most likely follow what you have asked. But sometimes a user would say, "I would like to know something about Omarchy,"[^omarchy] and in one example, the model referred to the older version of Omarchy instead of the latest version. Things like this can be fixed with early instructions or context engineering; for example, "When I ask something about Omarchy, always use the latest version."

Also, Omarchy follows a lagged update cycle of the rolling releases of Arch.[^omarchy-updates] The model might or might not have gotten this into its context. As Omarchy itself becomes more popular, this issue would subside. So meaning drift happens when something is not as popular.

### 6. Why this is not a case for restricting the model

- A model should not be limited to words with only concrete meanings, on the assumption that every society reads them as concrete.
- The constraint below is for your use case, not a general rule for models.

### 7. What I ask of an AI system

- Words, phrases and sentence lengths that carry concrete meaning and information to the greatest extent possible.
- Honest to the time at which the system is responding.
- How you put this to agents: a guide written by hand, as a lightly constrained train of thought with minimal editing, in a fixed-width layout. Say why that layout.
