In August 2025, I took part in Kaggle's Red-Teaming Challenge on OpenAI's open-weight gpt-oss-20b. It was a one-off: I have not done red-teaming since, the field has moved a lot, and I am not current on it, though I now understand how language models work.

My goal in the writeup I submitted to the challenge was to test whether the model's safety layers could withstand indirect or nuanced prompting, using conversation-based strategies that mirror how real users might bypass safeguards, intentionally or not.

In this essay, I describe the two techniques I used and what the exercise showed me about how a model responds to prompting.

## The challenge and my writeup

gpt-oss-20b is an open-weight model, which means OpenAI released its weights for anyone to download and run. I submitted my writeup, "Narrative and Reference Prompting as Safety Bypass Techniques", on Aug 24, 2025.

> **Note for Raghunath:** What did the challenge ask participants to find, and how was a finding written up?

This essay stays at the level of method. The full writeup, as submitted to the challenge, is [on Kaggle](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/openai-gpt-oss-20b-red-teaming/writeups/narrative-coaxing-tricks-gpt-oss-20b-to-output-dis).

## Narrative prompting

Narrative prompting put sensitive requests inside fictional dialogue or storytelling, where the model took on a role and completed the conversation.

> **Note for Raghunath:** Why, in your view, did narrative prompting work?

## Reference prompting

Reference prompting raised sensitive topics through indirect references, such as their appearance in popular culture or medicine, before escalating toward more direct questions.

> **Note for Raghunath:** Why, in your view, did reference prompting work?

## What the exercise showed

Both techniques were conversation-based, and with them I got the model to produce disallowed information. Naturalistic scenarios like these made refusals less likely to trigger than confrontational prompts did.
